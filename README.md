# App-Code-Design

What were the requirements and goals of your app?

I developed an inventory app that lets users create an account, log in, and manage their items and quantities. The goal was to give users a simple way to keep track of inventory and know when something runs out.

What screens and features supported user needs?

My app included a login screen, an inventory screen, and a notifications screen. I kept the layout simple with clear labels and buttons for adding, editing, and deleting items. This made it easy to find what was needed without having to go through extra steps.

How did you approach coding your app?

I worked on one feature at a time, starting with the database and login before moving into inventory management and notifications. Breaking it into smaller parts made problems easier to find and fix. I can use this same approach in future projects to stay organized.

How did you test your app, and why was testing important?

I used the Android Studio emulator to test creating an account, logging in, and adding, editing, and deleting inventory. Testing helped me check that the app worked as expected and catch problems before finishing it. It also helped me find and fix issues with the login and inventory display.

Where did you overcome a challenge during development?

One challenge was getting the inventory screen to show updated information after changes. I handled this by refreshing the table after items were added, edited, or deleted. This kept the screen consistent with what was saved in the database.

What part of your app best demonstrated your skills?

I think the inventory management feature was my strongest part. It connected the SQLite database to the interface and let users add, edit, and delete their own items. It showed that I could take an app idea and turn it into something functional.
